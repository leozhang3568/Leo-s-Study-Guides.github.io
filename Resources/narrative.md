<div style="text-indent: 0in; margin-bottom: 2em;">
Leo Zhang<br>Professor Marna<br>Bay Area Writing Project<br>3 August 2026
</div>

As I dragged my small body up the last flight of stairs, there was a familiar smell of old paint and wood. My shoes, although soft-soled, still made a glassy sound on the glossy floor, no matter how hard I tried to soften my gait. Peering at the choppy wall, I started to lead my fingers to it, trying to feel its bumpy surface, but I quickly withdrew them, remembering that I was not supposed to touch random, foreign surfaces. 

I stopped at a door and knocked quietly. 

As the door squeaked open and I looked inside, I saw my grandma, who was peering out of her thick glasses half-held by the end of her nasal bone. She had on the same black, wavy dress that she always wore, and her hair was dyed black and curled uniformly as always. 

There was a neat, spacious, and impeccable bed in the center of her room and a TV counter on the right side, although I did not see any TV. Instead, it was filled with the most oddly shaped antiques that I had ever seen. I could feel its wooden, smooth, and dark brown surface. Walking along it, I noticed a small, budget dining table near the back of the room and an air purifier next to the TV counter. I had always known that Grandma was a person with special attention to health. She took many assorted vitamins and minerals every day and made sure that her environment was clean. Even so, she said, it had not been enough to prevent random cleanliness-related "phenomenons" from happening.

"Every time I lay down on this bed and close my eyes," she said, "I can instantly hear little squeaks all around me."

I burst out laughing. I've never heard of mice behaving like this.

"Mice," she stated, "can be treated with just a couple squirts of this cleaning solution."

She showed me a bottle with a green liquid in it. Immediately, questions arose in my consciousness. If she already knew how to get rid of those "ugly creatures", then why were they still squeaking in her sleep? Oh man! My excitement level went up. I told her to lay on her bed and close her eyes. I gradually made my senses receptive. I saw nothing. I smelled nothing. I heard nothing. Well, except the air purifier buzzing around like a cranky, warped airplane propeller.

After a while, I found myself staring at a plate of unpleasant food on the table. Me, being five years old, was never excited to "consume" food. I made sure that I had not the slightest hint of excitement at food. Grandma insisted on my eating, but I was stubborn. I watched the clock. Crank, crank, crank. I waited for an unreasonable time. Finally, I began nibbling at the stone-cold food like a tiny bunny. Grandma was still there, watching. She chewed one sunflower seed. I loosened my jaw. She chewed another. I tightened my jaw. Again and again. By this ratio of two to one, I ate. 

Suddenly, I became aware of my surroundings again, since Grandma was saying something. I expected her to praise me on how quickly I ate, but to my surprise, she scolded me for not only not eating, but somehow adding food to my plate. My mind was filled with explosions of disgusting words that I could have, but didn't, say to her. No matter how hard I tried expediting my consumption of the matter on the table, she was eating sunflower seeds at the rate of two sunflower seeds in about five seconds. I liked the order, the ratio, and the perfect synchronization of sounds. Grandma was like a metronome going at twenty-four beats per minute.

After perhaps a couple of years, I finally . . . didn't finish. My Dad appeared at the doorway. A random thought came. My eyes set on a wooden table. (Wait what? Why is everything wooden?) I placed one hand, then another. I gradually mustered strength. On the table, I gained a new perspective. My Dad became a colleague, a coworker, and a best friend at the same time. I raised my fists into the air.

Feeling accomplished, I returned home with my "colleague", concluding my interesting visit to my Grandma's mysterious dwelling.
