## Discussion Questions

### Ch. 3

1. Compare and contrast indentured servitude with slavery.

    - Indentured servitude is where a person is bound to work for a master without recompense for a specific amount of time, after which he is free.

    - Slavery treats people as property, and the status could be passed down.

2. What were the differences between Barbados’s and Virginia’s use of slaves? Why did Virginia resist becoming like Barbados?

    - Barbados mostly had black slaves because English planters there wanted to make their fortunes and leave, but in Virginia, planters brought families, and they wanted an English society.

3. At first, there were both white and black indentured servants. How, if at all, were they treated differently? 

    - Blacks were forbidden to be armed, and periods of indenture were longer for them. Blacks were punished more severely.

4. What was Bacon’s Rebellion? Why were landowners so threatened by it? 

    - Bacon's Rebellion was an uprising in 1676 during which black and white indentured servants revolted agains thte Virginian central government.
    - Landowners were threatened because they realized how powerful the societal class could make the servants.

5. If you wanted to make sure that slaves stayed on the lower rungs of society, what laws would you create and enact? 

    - I would enact laws that prevented slaves from voting, laws that prevented them from owning weapons, and laws that prevented them from receiving proper education.

6. Explain Jefferson’s “the ears of the wolf” metaphor. 

    - The government could neither hold on to slavery, nor safely let it go.

7. How do you explain the profound paradox of how Jefferson lived and what he thought about slavery?

    - Jefferson himself owned slaves, although he believed that it was immoral to do so. He thought that slavery should be abolished but all African Americans had to be removed from society. Jefferson still held on to the belief that blacks were inferior to whites.

### Ch. 13

1. In what ways was the life of a sharecropper different from or like the life of a slave?  

    - The life of a sharecropper has more freedom than that of a slave, but it still trapped Black sharecroppers in cycles of debt.

2. How had black people in the South changed since emancipation?   

    - Black people in the South were eager to migrate to the North. In the South, they were tortured by infinite debts. They were enslaved all the same.

3. How did many white southerners regard young blacks in the early twentieth century? Why?   

    - Many white southerns disliked the attitudes of young Blacks because they had never experienced slavery. Furthermore, they feared that they would not be humble anymore.

4. How did African American migrants counter the racism they found in Chicago?   

    - In order to deal with the repeated attempts of evicting them from neighborhoods, African Americans started to promote black solidarity and built their own communities consisting of banks, insurance companies, and stores.

5. Describe the demographic changes of Harlem from 1790 (1890?) to the present.   

    - From 1890, Blacks migrated into Harlem by masses, creating the "Harlem Renaissance". It became an enclave filled with aspiring Blacks who wanted change. Starting from the 1950s, though, Black populations dwindled due to housing discriminations.

6. How did the Great Depression affect various races and classes, especially African Americans?   

    - The Great Depression dropped the viel of glamor, imposing poverty among all classes. African Americans were especially affected because they were already at the bottom of the social class. When they moved to southern cities to find work, they were met with unemployed white mobs. In 1932, unemployment among Blacks rose to 50%.

7. Why did unemployment rates differ between blacks and whites?   

    - Whites were given priority by employers. Blacks were "last to be hired and the first to be fired".

8. Discuss the roles of unions in ending racial discrimination.   

    - Unions allowed people of different races to band together. For example, the United Auto Workers won wage increases as well as union recognition.

9. Explain the widespread shift of black Americans from Republicans to Democrats.

    - Previously sided with Republicans due to Lincoln's Emancipation Proclamation, Blacks shifted to Democrats because they were disillusioned with Herbert Hoover and the Republicans.

### Ch. 5

1. Describe the segregation of African Americans in the North, in both housing and labor.  

    - African Americans were denied housing in mostly white neighborhoods and were reduced to having to live in slums. Blacks were only allowed menial jobs.

2. How were African Americans treated socially and legally in the North?   

    - African Americans were attacked by white mobs and banned from intermarriage and suffrage by indirect, then direct, means. There were also segregated schools. They were also given negative racial images including "immature", "indolent", and "good for nothing".

3. Describe the working conditions for slaves in the South.  

    - Slaves lived in squalid, unkempt shelters, with poor hygene that barely kept them healthy.

4. Describe three methods that enslavers used to ensure that slaves would continue to provide labor and not rebel.  

    - They used **kindness**, **strict discipline**, and **psychological control**.

5. What were the two contrasting views Southern whites held about slaves.   

    - Southern whites believed that slaves were childlike ("Sambo"), but they also "cherished the bonds of affection".
6. Why is Nat Turner notable?  

    - Nat Turner was humble and docile, but on August 22, 1831 in Virginia, Turner led an insurrection, killing sixty whites. His reason of attack was not hatred. It was because he had a spiritual vision. 
    - After this incident, white southerners realized that "Sambos" could be secretly planning destruction.

7. How did urban slavery differ from that of the plantations? How and why did it alter or affect the system of slavery?   

    - Urban slavery inslaving some African Americans in urban areas rather than rural, agriculture-based places. It made slavery less strict, and it also made Frederick Douglass realize that force was the only way to defeat slavery.

8. Describe Frederick Douglass’s early life.  

    - Frederick Douglass was influenced by a white woman named Sophia Auld to embrce freedom. After he was enslaved by Master Thomas Auld and put under slave breaker Edward Covey, Douglass struggled with keeping his vision of freedom. During a fight with Covey, he found out that force was the only way to fend for himself.

9. What was Covey supposed to do to Douglass? What ended up occurring instead?   

    - Covey was instructed to transform Douglass psychologically into an obedient slave. Instead, during a fight with Covey, he found out that force was the only way to fend for himself. 

10. Why did Abraham Lincoln not want to use black soldiers, and what made him change his mind?   

    - Abraham Lincoln feared that using Black soldiers would discourage whites to join, but manpower shortages rose in the spring of 1863, and the North was almost losing the war.

11. How did black soldiers fare in battle, and how did they affect the outcome of the war?   

    - Around one third of all Black soldiers died, but they still saved the North and allowed them to win the war.

12. What did William Sherman intend to do for blacks in the South?   

    - William Sherman wanted to grant forty acres of land to Blacks until Congress could settle the case.

13. What did the Southern farm owners (and former enslavers) intend to do to the newly freed blacks?   

    - They planned to enslave the slaves again.

14. Describe the sharecropping system.  

    - The sharecropping system involved a person working for a landowner in exchange for a portion of their crops.

15. How does the economy of the South change? How does it affect the free blacks?   

    - Through the production of cotton, textile, and iron production, the South became increasingly economically boosted, allowing the freed blacks to be involved in industrial work. Southern industrialists were also very willing to recruit Blacks.

16. Describe the treatment of African Americans towards the end of Reconstruction. 

    - They were confined by the Jim Crow system, lynched, and killed randomly. Many were forced in debt as sharecroppers or tenant farmers.
